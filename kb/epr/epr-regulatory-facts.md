# US packaging EPR – facts as of 21 Oct 2026

Always add "as of <date>" when using these in a post. Regulations and court cases change; re-check before reuse.

## Basics
- Packaging EPR shifts the cost of recycling packaging from local governments to producers. Obligated producers join a Producer Responsibility Organization (PRO), report packaging supply data and pay fees.
- **Seven states** have enacted packaging EPR laws: California, Colorado, Maine, Maryland, Minnesota, Oregon, Washington.
- **Circular Action Alliance (CAA)** is the approved PRO in six of the seven states.
- Fees are based on the amount and type of packaging supplied into each state; CAA builds a state budget, applies relative material cost indices, and charges producers on reported supply in state-specific categories.
- Several states use **eco-modulation**: fees go up or down based on packaging attributes (recyclability, recycled content).
- EY: early estimates suggest up to ~1% of sales per jurisdiction could go to EPR fees.
- No federal packaging EPR law; CAA is trying to harmonise categories and templates, but differences remain.
- Annual supply reports to CAA have been due **May 31** for the prior calendar year (2026 cycle).

## Oregon – Recycling Modernization Act (SB 582)
- First US packaging EPR program to go live; producer fees in effect since **1 July 2025**.
- Producer definition depends on brand ownership; for some packaging it is the first distributor into the state.
- Small producers below **one metric ton** of covered products into Oregon are exempt from fees.
- 2027 fee schedule expected October 2026.
- Litigation: per our own 2026 post, the federal district court upheld the law on 27 Aug 2026 and NAW appealed to the Ninth Circuit on 24 Sep 2026. (Re-verify before reuse.)

## Colorado – HB 22-1355
- Producer dues (Colorado calls them **dues**, not fees) started **January 2026**; full statewide program implementation **9 June 2026**.
- Exempts most business-to-business packaging.
- ~2,100 producers registered with CAA Colorado as of January 2026.
- Litigation: **NAW v. Ryan** (filed July 2026) challenges the law; these are NAW's arguments, not court findings.

## California – SB 54
- Covers single-use packaging and single-use plastic food serviceware. Most far-reaching US program.
- Permanent regulations approved **1 May 2026**.
- CAA submitted its revised final program plan to CalRecycle on **13 Oct 2026**. CalRecycle has **75 calendar days** to respond. Submission is not approval.
- Program launch targeted **1 January 2027**. Proposed fee rates only take effect if CalRecycle approves the plan.
- Revised plan budget: about **$1.11 billion for 2027** (down from up to $1.87 billion in the June draft); five-year range about $7.8–13.1 billion.
- More than 4,000 producers registered with CAA (per the plan).
- CAA estimated malus: **$100/ton in 2027, $150 in 2028, $200 in 2029**.
- SB 54 targets by 2032: 25% source reduction of single-use plastic packaging and foodware (vs 2023 baseline), 65% recycling rate for single-use plastic packaging/foodware, 100% of covered material recyclable or compostable.
- SB 54 requires $500 million a year for 10 years ($5 billion) for the California Plastic Pollution Mitigation Fund, starting 2027.
- Producers can join the PRO as one route to compliance (California allows voluntary PRO participation).
- Multiple lawsuits still aim to block SB 54.
