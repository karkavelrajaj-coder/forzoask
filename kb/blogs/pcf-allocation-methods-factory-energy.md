# PCF allocation methods – summary

**Core idea:** One meter often feeds many production lines making dozens of products. Turning shared energy into a per-product figure depends on the allocation method; the same plant data can give different per-unit results.

## Why energy is harder than materials
Energy is shared across equipment; metered at a different level than product output; includes facility loads (lighting, HVAC, offices, idle/standby).

Group factory energy first: **directly attributable process energy**, **shared process energy**, **general facility energy** – each may need a different approach.

## ISO 14044 hierarchy
1. Avoid allocation (subdivide the process or use system expansion).
2. Allocate by a physical relationship (mass, energy content) that reflects how flows arise.
3. Use another relationship (e.g. economic value) only if no suitable physical one exists.

## Methods compared
- Direct measurement / subdivision – best when energy can be attributed; metering gaps remain.
- Machine hours – fine when power draw is similar across products; misleading when loads/speeds differ.
- Mass or volume – simple; wrong when processing needs differ.
- Measured/estimated energy (power × time × load) – good with reliable equipment data.
- Floor area – a proxy for some facility loads like lighting; weak for HVAC.
- Economic value – last resort; results shift when relative prices change even if production doesn't.

## Choosing a key – 5 questions
Can you attribute directly? What drives consumption? Does demand differ by product? Is the data repeatable? Can you justify the choice?

## Common mistakes
One key for everything · changing methods between periods without explanation · picking the method that gives a nicer number · ignoring idle energy · mixing measured and estimated data without labelling · economic allocation without justification.
