# Top 10 Sections of a PCF Report (with checklist) – summary

**Core idea:** A kg CO₂e figure alone tells readers little. Customers, auditors and reviewers ask for the report behind it: what was assessed, which data and assumptions were used, and whether the result fits its intended use. ISO 14067 and the GHG Protocol Product Standard both stress transparent reporting. The 10 sections are a practical structure, not a template mandated by either standard.

## The 10 sections (question each answers)
1. Executive summary – result, unit, boundary, data period, method, review status.
2. Goal and intended use – why, for whom, internal vs customer vs public; comparisons need extra care.
3. Product and functional (or declared) unit – product, model/SKU, sites, reference flow, packaging treatment.
4. System boundary and life cycle stages – cradle-to-gate vs cradle-to-grave; list inclusions/exclusions and cut-offs.
5. Data sources and data quality – primary vs secondary data; source, period, geography, technology; flag proxies.
6. Calculation method and assumptions – activity data × emission factor; GWP factors, electricity, transport, scrap, recycling, allocation, biogenic carbon.
7. Results by stage and material – total first, then breakdowns and a short hotspot analysis; avoid false precision.
8. Uncertainty and sensitivity – which inputs move the result; don't claim a range you didn't calculate.
9. Limitations and exclusions – what the number does not cover; one configuration ≠ another.
10. Review status and document control – internal review vs critical review vs third-party verification; version and change history.

## Common mistakes
A number without a boundary · unclear data sources · undocumented assumptions · goal/method mismatch (screening result presented as a declaration) · missing version control · false precision.

## Key message
Traceability: the result and its main contributors should trace back to data, methods, assumptions and boundary.
