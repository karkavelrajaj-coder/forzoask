# Lifecycle stages that drive emissions – summary

**Core idea:** No single lifecycle stage is always the biggest. It depends on materials, processes, distribution, use profile, lifetime and end of life. Category averages don't give a specific product's footprint.

## Five stages (GHG Protocol Product Standard)
1. Material acquisition and pre-processing (incl. transport of inputs to the plant) – often outside your own facilities; supplier data quality matters.
2. Production – the stage you control most; needs allocation when a site makes many products (energy, process emissions, scrap, waste).
3. Distribution and storage – distance, mode, weight, storage conditions.
4. Use – can dominate for products using electricity or fuel; very sensitive to lifetime and energy-source assumptions.
5. End of life – collection, recycling, disposal; recycling allocation method must be documented.

## Rough patterns (starting points, not rules)
Low-energy products → materials/production; energy-using products → use phase; bio-based inputs → material acquisition; energy-intensive manufacturing → production.

## How to find hotspots (6 steps)
Define the functional unit → set the boundary → collect data per stage → document allocation → review stage-level results → test key assumptions.

## Common mistakes
Treating a category average as your product · ignoring the use profile · comparing cradle-to-gate with cradle-to-grave · assuming one stage always dominates · inconsistent allocation · reporting only the total.
